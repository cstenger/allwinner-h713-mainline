# Handoff — 2026-08-29

Branch `h713-display-video-path` (renamed from `h713-dvfs-1416-corruption` at the
end of this session — the name had long since stopped describing the contents;
earlier handoffs still cite the old name and are left as dated records).
Board healthy, back on our stack and booting normally end to end: U-Boot logo on
the panel, Linux console, Debian login.
Companion to [`plane-brief-for-external-review.md`](plane-brief-for-external-review.md),
whose §0 this session was written to answer.

The previous session ended by saying the obvious next step was to diff the
configured-but-black register dump against a stock Android playback capture.
This session did that, and then kept going: every difference the diff turned up
was forced onto working stock hardware to see which of them actually matters.

**Headline: none of them causes the black — and that is the useful result.**
Two further findings that came out sideways are worth more than the original
question.

## The method, and why it is worth reusing

Instead of setting values on the broken system and hoping, set them on the
**working** one and watch it break. A working system that fails when you remove
one bit tells you that bit is load-bearing; a broken system that stays broken
when you add it tells you almost nothing.

Every probe restored the value it read at run time, so a phase that blanked the
panel recovered by itself, and every phase **polled** rather than slept — 300
samples over six seconds — to prove the write actually *persisted* rather than
merely landing. That polling is what caught the biggest trap of the session.

Tools: [`tools/display/hidtvreg-dump.c`](../tools/display/hidtvreg-dump.c) (fixed
capture), [`hidtvreg-read.c`](../tools/display/hidtvreg-read.c) (arbitrary window,
stock), [`hidtvreg-poke.c`](../tools/display/hidtvreg-poke.c) (the probes),
[`mmio-read.c`](../tools/display/mmio-read.c) (arbitrary window, our arm64 Linux),
[`mmio-poke.c`](../tools/display/mmio-poke.c) (arm64 writer, `/dev/mem`).

## THE RULE: writes to AFBD are inert until the commit latch is pulsed

Control at `+0x00`, latch at `+0x04`. Clearing the source enable at `0x05600010`
and holding it — verified held, 300/300 samples, never reverted by the firmware —
left playing video **completely unaffected**. The *identical* write with
`0x05600014` pulsed replaced the video with **solid green**.

**An uncommitted write lands in the register, reads back, holds indefinitely, and
does nothing. Readback proves nothing on this block.** Any earlier result resting
on "the write stuck" without a latch pulse deserves re-checking.

Green rather than black is itself diagnostic: `Y=0, Cb=0, Cr=0` is about
`RGB(0,135,0)`. Composition dropped to zero luma, which confirms **AFBD source 0
is genuinely the live video path to the panel** — the positive control this line
of work never had.

## Every stock-diff difference, forced onto working hardware

| difference | forced onto stock | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bit 31 on `0x05600100`/`0x05600140` | cleared, committed, singly and together | **no effect** |
| fmt 4 rather than stock's fmt 0 | set, committed | tiled, half-height, colour-shifted |
| IOMMU master 2 bypassed | `0x02010030` bit 2 set | **coloured static** |
| mixer control `0x1003` | written, ±latch | no effect |
| mixer control zeroed entirely | written, ±latch | **no effect** |

**None produces black.** Two produce visible corruption; the rest do nothing.

**fmt 4 is causal but not the black.** Photographed off the panel: the image
repeated roughly twice horizontally, squashed into the top ~45%, flat below,
colours shifted. One signature, not two — the fetch consumes about two source
lines per output line, so content repeats across the width and exhausts the
buffer at half height. The flat region was a *different* colour in two successive
photographs (teal, then yellow-green), i.e. fetch past valid data. Chroma is
misread as well as stride. So stock's fmt 0 is correct for NV12 and making DECD's
VideoInfo produce it is a real fix — but fmt 4 here is *plainly visible*, while
our state shows nothing. **Necessary, not sufficient.**

**The mixer is not in the video path at all.** Zeroing its layer control outright,
with and without a `GLOBAL_DBUFF`-style latch at `0x0525c008`, changes nothing.
That agrees with the block sweep, which found none of its registers moving during
playback.

### Why the negative is worth having

On stock, **every** way source 0 can be broken still puts *something* on the
panel: green when starved, tiling when misformatted, static when misaddressed.
The path downstream of source 0 passes whatever it produces, unconditionally.
Ours shows nothing at all. So the black is **not a misconfiguration of source 0**
— it is source 0's output not reaching composition. That was already the
conclusion from the blue-generator test; it now has hardware support arrived at
from the opposite direction.

## Two results that outlast the register hunt

**The GPU is genuinely off during video.** Mali runtime PM counters:

```
idle, 12 s                        active +0 ms      suspended +12114 ms
playing, controls visible, 12 s   active +12128 ms  suspended +0 ms
playing, controls hidden, 15 s    active +0 ms      suspended +15115 ms
```

The middle row nearly produced the opposite conclusion. Measured alone it says
stock composites video on the GPU, which would undercut this project's premise.
It is the player's transport controls. Once they auto-hide with video still
playing, the GPU is **completely suspended**. The premise — vendor puts video on
the panel with no GPU colour conversion — is now **confirmed on hardware** rather
than inferred from `hwcomposer`.

This also answers the subtitle/OSD question the brief carries as an open product
risk. Stock has two modes: video alone runs decoder → dma-buf → AFBD source 0 →
panel with the GPU asleep, and video *with* UI wakes the GPU to composite both
into a single buffer that AFBD scans out. That is why the transport controls
appeared tiled *inside* source 0's corruption in the fmt-4 photographs. **The
vendor answer to overlays is wake the GPU and composite, not hardware subtitle
blending.**

**Only AFBD is driven per frame.** Sweeping eleven windows at idle twice (to
separate free-running counters) and once during playback:

| block | video-driven registers |
| --- | --- |
| **AFBD** | **8** — the Y bases `0x05600070`-`7c` and C bases `0x05600084`-`90`, flipping as a ring |
| TVTOP, MIXER, DE_OSD, LAYER, ROUTE, LVDS_PHY, PLL, GE2D, MIPS, IOMMU | **0** |

**TVTOP is not driven for video** — its 48 registers are byte-identical idle
versus playback. But *static is not unused*: TVTOP still routes, the LVDS PHY
still drives the panel, the PLL still clocks it. They are load-bearing and
**configured once at init**. The implementation scope is therefore: get those
right once, and the only per-frame driving needed is AFBD's buffer slots.

## The comparison that had never been made

Booted back to our stack and swept the same eleven windows.
**49 of 340 registers differ.** `TVTOP`, `LAYER` and `ROUTE` are **byte-identical**
to stock, independently confirming they are already correct on our side.

**Cleanest finding — our H/V total is off by one, in two independent blocks:**

```
MIXER   0x0525c000   stock 0x02F7054F   ours 0x02F80550
DE_OSD  0x0524c010   stock 0x02F7054F   ours 0x02F80550
```

`0x54F` = 1359 against our 1360, `0x2F7` = 759 against our 760. Stock encodes
total-minus-one; we encode the total. As a lone mixer value this was dismissible.
A second independent block carrying the identical discrepancy makes it look like
a **convention error worth fixing**.

**It was tried, and it blanks the panel. These totals are coupled across
blocks.** U-Boot commit `cbdc6d6e90a`, reverted in `8fe568cdfc4`:

- Both registers took the stock value: `0x02F7054F` on hardware afterwards, and
  a re-sweep went from **49 differing registers to 47** — the two intended, zero
  newly differing. The change did exactly what it was meant to.
- **The projector went black.** The revert restored it. Both confirmed visually.

`0x05880020` carries the same word and was deliberately left at 1360x760 because
stock's value there had never been captured. So the mixer and DE described a
1359x759 raster while the TCON described 1360x760, and a raster whose blocks
disagree produces no output. Our set at 1360x760 is self-consistent and works;
stock's is presumably self-consistent at 1359x759 and also works. **Changing a
subset is worse than changing none.**

The caution that left `0x05880020` alone — do not match a register you have never
measured — is precisely what produced the breakage. The lesson is not "be less
cautious" but **"these three move together"**: capture `0x05880020` from stock and
change all three, or change nothing.

And a signature worth noting: U-Boot printed `boot logo published and committed`
on the boot with the black panel. That is the same reports-success-shows-nothing
behaviour the DECD work has been chasing, **reproduced here by a pure raster
mismatch with no DECD involved** — evidence that the signature is not specific to
the video path.

**Largest new difference — the LVDS PHY, 4 of 16, the block that drives the
panel:**

```
0x051c0010   stock 0x00000000   ours 0x00000045
0x051c0014   stock 0x1A000005   ours 0x18000005
0x051c0024   stock 0x00350000   ours 0x00300000
0x051c0028   stock 0x08100035   ours 0x1F300030
```

**Recorded with its counter-evidence.** Our values are exactly what `devmem32.c`
and the handoff doc record as the *working logo* configuration, so they
demonstrably drive this panel; and stock has the MIPS owning the display where
ours has U-Boot. Two valid configurations fits as well as one broken one.
Strongest new lead, **not** a smoking gun.

**Confirmed visually at the end of this session**, which settles that caveat: on
a normal boot the operator sees the U-Boot boot logo on the panel, then the Linux
console, then the login prompt — with these PHY values live and `card0-LVDS-1`
enumerated. **Our LVDS PHY configuration drives this panel correctly.** So the
four differences are a real divergence from stock but not a fault, and chasing
them should be ranked below the total-minus-one encoding.

**IOMMU, now concrete rather than inferred:** our bypass register reads
`0x0000007C` — masters 2–6, and master 2 is `dec@5600000` — against stock's zero,
with a different TTB (`0x41F28000` vs `0x6F3A0000`) and TLB prefetch. Bypass was
already shown to give static rather than black, so it stays a difference, not a
cause.

Also: `PLL 0x058c0018` is `0xC950311E` on ours and **zero** on stock; `GE2D
0x05240018` is `0x00004000` on stock and zero on ours.

**State caveat:** stock was playing video; ours was an idle console with the MIPS
parked (`0x0306101c` = 0 vs 1) and no DECD activity, so AFBD's 37 differences are
expected. A capture in the actual configured-but-black DECD state would be
strictly better and needs the 0068/0070 kernel installed.

## Corrections to things believed earlier

- **"Setting bit 31 is the fix."** Eliminated. It differs, and it does nothing.
- **"The mixer `0x0525c004` is the strongest lead."** It is not in the video path.
- **"Phase 4's null shows the Android UI is not composited through AFBD."** Wrong
  — that write was uncommitted, so it tested nothing. Still untested.
- **"The commit latch explains seven sessions of ineffective ARM-side writes."**
  Overstated. The brief records the Linux session *did* arm `0x05600014`. It
  explains this session's nulls, not the history.
- **"The GPU is active during playback, so stock composites on it."** Wrong, and
  it was one measurement away from being recorded as fact. It was the player UI.
- **"The restore SPL is stale and must be regenerated."** It carried an Aug 25
  first stage against eMMC's Aug 28 U-Boot proper and booted through to Debian.
  Regenerating from the newest build could *create* a mismatch instead.

## Operational notes, each of which cost time here

- **`sunxi-fel -p uboot` does not work on this SoC.** It loads SPL, BL31 and
  U-Boot correctly, then fails the AArch64 warm-reset handoff (`RVBAR
  0x08100040`), goes silent, and drops back to the BROM. `docs/flash.md` only
  ever documents `-p spl` for good reason: the restore SPL is a **32-bit** SPL
  running in SRAM that writes LBA `0x10` directly and never needs that handoff.
- **The vendor player silently refuses a video-only file** — "Video Problem: Do
  not support this video". The byte-identical video muxed with a silent AAC track
  plays immediately.
- **Files pushed to `/sdcard` land `-rw-------`** under a FUSE-synthesised uid
  that `chmod` cannot change, so a `file://` path is unopenable. Scan with
  `MEDIA_SCANNER_SCAN_FILE` and launch the resulting `content://` URI.
- **`pidof`/`ps` name-matching does not find the player** on this build even while
  it is running. Trust logcat.
- **Verify the test clip contains motion before it reaches the board.** A clip
  generated as flat navy with a `drawbox` that silently never rendered made
  "playing" and "frozen" indistinguishable and wasted an observation. Hash frames
  at several timestamps and confirm they differ.
- **`adb screencap` cannot capture the video.** It reads the Android surface; the
  video is on a hardware overlay that never appears there. Photograph the panel.
- **Match the reference's output format exactly.** The first stock capture emitted
  upper-case addresses against a lower-case reference: 90 of 150 lines came back
  changed on formatting alone. Both readers here reproduce the reference
  byte-for-byte on purpose.
- **`adb shell` inside a `while read` loop eats the loop's stdin.** Redirect it
  from `/dev/null` or the sweep silently captures only its first window.
- **`pkill -f "<pattern>"` matches its own command line** and kills the shell
  running it. Same family as the `pgrep` self-match already in the notes.

## The black state itself, finally swept (2026-08-29, later)

The earlier 49-register comparison was idle-vs-playing, so most of it was state
rather than fault. This closes that gap: the configured-but-black DECD state was
reproduced and swept directly.

Reproducing it: 0068 kernel (DECD exclusive, KMS disabled), `preboot` set to
`h713_disp init 0x34` so the MIPS firmware stays alive, one NV12 frame submitted
through `/dev/decd`. Confirmed in-state by `0x05600010 = 0x03000413`,
`0x05600020 = 0x02CF04FF`, `0x05600040 = 0x00000500`, `0x0560004c = 0x02D00500`
-- byte-identical to the 2026-08-28 reference dump. Capture in
[reference/linux-black-state-sweep-2026-08-29.txt](reference/linux-black-state-sweep-2026-08-29.txt).

**41 of 340 registers differ from stock. The decisive part is which ones do not.**

Every difference outside AFBD -- the LVDS PHY four, the PLL, GE2D, the mixer and
DE totals, the IOMMU bypass -- is **byte-identical between the black state and
our idle state**. Our idle state has a *working panel*. So every one of those is
demonstrably compatible with a working display and is eliminated as a cause,
including the LVDS PHY four that the earlier sweep had made the headline lead.

**That puts the entire fault inside AFBD**, in 13 non-volatile registers. The
standout was a mirror image:

```
             STOCK         OURS (black)
0x05600100   0x83001901    0x00010000     enabled + configured  vs  empty
0x05600104   0x00000001    0x00000000     commit latch set      vs  clear
0x05600108   0x008000FF    0x00000000
0x0560010c   0x00FF0080    0x00000000
0x05600140   0x83001900    0x03001901     bit 0 CLEAR           vs  SET
```

Stock services channel `0x05600100` and leaves `0x140` disabled; we do the exact
opposite. That suggested a clean model: source 0 is the video producer, these
channels are the consumers, stock enables `0x100` to consume the video, and we
enable `0x140` for our OSD -- which is why our logo and console render -- leaving
source 0's output composited by nobody.

### The model was tested and is REFUTED

`tools/display/mmio-poke.c` wrote stock's `0x100` configuration verbatim,
disabled `0x140`, pulsed the latch, and the caller then submitted a frame into
it. Readback confirmed every write took: `0x05600100 = 0x83001901`,
`0x05600108 = 0x008000FF`, `0x0560010c = 0x00FF0080`, `0x05600140 = 0x03001900`,
latch `= 1`. A separate foreground run held all of it for 1000/1000 samples
across 20 seconds without reverting.

**The panel did not change. It stayed white throughout.**

And it did not even go *black* when `0x140`'s enable was cleared, which is the
more informative half. If AFBD were driving the panel, disabling the enabled
channel should have changed something. It did not.

### What that points at instead

**CORRECTED 2026-08-30 — the first version of this section was wrong, and it was
committed and pushed before the error was caught.**

It claimed DECD was submitting into an AFBD stage that was not cycling, on the
grounds that `0x05600058`/`0x0560005c` sat at `0x3B`/`0x46` across three samples
while stock's advanced during playback. Both halves of that are false:

- In **our idle state, which has a working panel**, those registers read
  **`0x00000000`** — verified live, five samples over five seconds, with the logo
  and console rendering. A register that is zero on a working display cannot be a
  liveness indicator for it.
- Stock's never advanced monotonically either. Re-reading the samples, they
  oscillate between `0x47` and `0x6C`. Low variance was mistaken for "frozen".

What the values actually say is the opposite of the claim: they are **source-0
video-path** counters, zero when source 0 is disabled (idle), non-zero when it is
enabled and submitting (black state, stock playback). So in the black state
source 0 **is** active, which agrees with DECD's 13155 interrupts and with the
existing conclusion that source 0 does its job and its output does not arrive.

**The counters therefore yield no new lead.** They are consistent with everything
already known and discriminate nothing.

What survives from this run is the *poke* result, which does not depend on the
counters: configuring channel `0x05600100` exactly as stock has it, disabling
`0x140`, latched and confirmed by readback, changed nothing on the panel — and
clearing `0x140`'s enable did not even blank it. In our black state the panel is
**frozen**: it does not respond to disabling the channel that is supposedly
feeding it.

That is worth contrasting with the earlier sessions' black-state runs, which
report the U-Boot logo visible on the panel *until deliberately hidden* — i.e.
scanout demonstrably running. Our run booted with `preboot h713_disp init 0x34`,
which initialises the display but publishes no logo, so the OSD channel had no
valid content and the panel showed uninitialised memory. Whether scanout was
running at all in our run is therefore **untested**, and it is the thing to
establish before any further consumer-side experiment: a frozen panel makes every
such experiment unfalsifiable, which may be exactly what has been happening.

### Operational note

This state hard-locks the SoC within seconds to tens of seconds, which your notes
already record. It locked three times during this session. What works is putting
the whole experiment in one on-board script that logs to the rootfs and does not
background anything: a lock then costs a power cycle instead of the result. The
sweep survived that way; two runs collected over the serial console did not.

## Where to go next

1. **Establish whether scanout is running at all in the black state.** This
   replaces the "AFBD counters are frozen" item, which rested on a misreading —
   see the correction above. The live question is different and sharper: in our
   black-state run the panel did not respond to disabling the channel that should
   be feeding it, so the image may simply be frozen. Earlier sessions report the
   U-Boot logo visible in *their* black state until deliberately hidden, so
   scanout was running for them; ours booted `init 0x34`, which publishes no logo,
   so we never established it. **A frozen panel makes every consumer-side
   experiment unfalsifiable**, which may be why a dozen of them have come back
   null. Boot the black state with `auto 0x34 logo` instead so there is a known
   image on the panel, confirm it is visible, and only then submit a frame.

2. **Capture `0x05880020` from stock**, then decide the total-minus-one question.
   Changing the mixer and DE alone blanks the panel (tried, reverted -- see
   above); the three registers are coupled. If stock reads `0x02F7054F` there too,
   it becomes a three-register change worth retrying. A measurement task, not a
   code change.

3. **Make DECD's VideoInfo produce fmt 0.** A real defect, proven causal for
   correct rendering -- but known not to be sufficient for the black, so it should
   land after the black is understood rather than before.

**Closed, so nobody re-opens them:**
